# Prohibited and Restricted Words in Listings — Enforcement Reference

Source: consolidated from Amazon listing policy, restricted products rules, and FTC claim
guidance enforced by Amazon catalog quality teams.

## High-Risk Claim Words (Require Substantiation or Registration)

Words in this section frequently trigger listing takedowns, stranded offers, or account-level
complaints when used without supporting documentation.

### Medical / therapeutic claims

- "cure", "heal", "treat", "remedy", "prevents disease", "eliminates pain"
- Rationale: an accessory or supplement claiming to treat or prevent disease is classified as
  an unapproved drug under FDA rules. Amazon suppresses these listings on sight.
- Safe alternative: describe comfort and support factually — "provides joint support" is
  allowed for pet products with adequate substantiation; "cures arthritis" is not.

### Antimicrobial claims

- "antibacterial", "antimicrobial", "kills germs", "disinfectant", "odor-eliminating
  technology that kills bacteria"
- Rationale: products making public-health claims are pesticides under FIFRA and require EPA
  registration numbers. An unregistered antibacterial claim is a federal labeling violation.
- Safe alternative: "odor-resistant material" or "easy-to-clean surface" without germ-kill
  claims.

### FDA claims

- "FDA approved", "FDA certified"
- Rationale: FDA does not "approve" facilities or most pet accessories; it registers food
  facilities. Only devices that went through premarket approval may use the phrase.
- Safe alternative: "Made in an FDA-registered facility" when true and documented.

## Prohibited Marketing Phrases (Auto-Block List)

The following phrases are blocked by listing quality automation regardless of substantiation:

- "Free shipping", "100% satisfaction guaranteed", "Money-back guarantee" (in title/bullets)
- "Best", "Bestseller", "#1", "Top rated", "Cheapest", "Lowest price", "Most popular"
- "Amazon's Choice", "Best Seller badge", or any reference to Amazon badges or awards
- "Hot sale", "Limited time offer", "Buy now", "While supplies last" in title or bullets
- "Cure", "miracle", "guaranteed results" in any field

## Comparison and Competitor References

- Naming a competitor brand or claiming superiority over it ("better than Kong") is not
  allowed in any listing field and may additionally trigger an IP complaint.
- Trademarked terms of other brands may not be used as search terms in the backend keyword
  field. This is the single most common cause of intellectual property complaints in the
  Pet Supplies category.

## Environmental Claims

- Unqualified claims such as "eco-friendly", "green", "sustainable" are flagged per FTC Green
  Guides unless qualified. "Recyclable packaging" must state whether facilities are broadly
  available.

## Enforcement Consequences

| Violation type | Typical first enforcement | Repeat enforcement |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibited marketing phrase | Listing suppressed, fix within warning window | Listing stranded |
| Medical/therapeutic claim | Listing takedown + document request | Account health flag |
| Antimicrobial claim without EPA number | Restricted products takedown | Account suspension risk |
| Competitor trademark in keywords | IP complaint from rights owner | Account suspension risk |

## Practical Pre-Flight Check (used by our compliance tooling)

Before submitting a new or updated listing, scan title, bullets, description, and backend
keywords for the blocked list above. Any hit must be either rewritten or backed by a document
(EPA registration number, substantiation file) before submission. Log every flagged word and
resolution in the listing change log for audit.
