# Pet Supplies Compliance Quick Reference — U.S. Marketplace

Source: Amazon Seller Central — Pet Supplies category requirements, CPSC guidance, and common
listing mistakes observed by account health teams.

## Children's Product Rule (Critical for Pet Toys and Apparel)

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) applies children's product rules to items
designed or intended primarily for children 12 years of age or younger. Pet products generally
fall outside this rule unless their design, marketing, or packaging appeals to children.

- If a pet toy contains small parts that could also be used by a child under 3, it may be
  subject to small parts testing under 16 CFR Part 1501.
- Listings that market a pet product to children ("great for kids", "children can play too")
  may trigger children's product compliance requirements, including CPSIA testing and a
  Children's Product Certificate (CPC).
- A CPC is required for children's products; it is not required for pure pet products, but many
  sellers voluntarily test pet toys to ASTM F963 standards to defend against customer safety
  complaints.

## Chew Toys and Durability Claims

- Do not claim a toy is "indestructible" unless you have independent third-party test data.
  "Indestructible" is treated as a performance claim and is frequently cited in product safety
  complaints.
- Use "designed for aggressive chewers" or "durable cotton rope construction" instead.
- Include a supervision disclaimer for hard chews and rope toys: "Always supervise your pet
  during play and remove toy if pieces break off."

## Pet Food and Treats

- Pet food and treats are regulated by the FDA and must comply with the Federal Food, Drug, and
  Cosmetic Act.
- Labels must list ingredients in descending order by weight, guaranteed analysis (crude protein,
  fat, fiber, moisture), and calorie content.
- Health-related claims such as "supports joint health" are structure/function claims and require
  substantiation; disease claims such as "cures arthritis" are prohibited.
- Imported pet food must comply with FDA foreign supplier verification rules and U.S. Customs
  requirements.

## Apparel, Collars, and Harnesses

- Apparel must include fiber content labels and care instructions in the images or A+ Content if
  not physically sewn in.
- Collars and harnesses must list maximum weight and girth ranges and display a size chart image.
- Reflective or LED accessories must state battery type and runtime; claims such as "waterproof"
  must specify the IP rating if available.

## Documentation Readiness

Keep the following documents available in case of an account health inquiry or customer complaint:

- Product test reports (ASTM F963, CPSIA component testing if applicable, flammability if apparel).
- Supplier declarations and material safety data sheets.
- Product liability insurance certificate.
- Recall and incident response plan.

## Common Listing Suppression Triggers for Pet Supplies

| Trigger | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Small parts warning missing | Squeaky toy with removable squeaker | Add "not for children under 3" and supervision note |
| Unsubstantiated durability claim | "Indestructible" | Replace with "designed for aggressive chewers" |
| Missing size chart | Dog harness without girth guide | Add size chart image and breed-weight reference |
| FDA/EPA claim misuse | "Antibacterial dog bowl" | Rewrite as "easy-to-clean, food-safe material" |
